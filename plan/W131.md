This is a career decision week — the hinge of the entire Phase-7 landing — not a Build week. There is no crate, no upstream mirror, and no engineered trap-test arc; manufacturing one would be padding. Every item below instead carries an explicit artifact file (offer details stay private; only tier-level sums and decision rationale are recorded) + an Output line that proves it done. The negotiation, comparison, and decision items each carry a 🎯 hardened-acceptance pass criterion and a *Mental model* of why the item exists now. The week's job: convert the offers in hand into one accepted offer scored against pre-committed criteria — without moving the goal-posts, without a same-day yes, and without burning the bridge you'll walk back across if the landing fails.

**Monday — Initial offers in**

- [ ] Artifact: `notes/W131_offers_inventory.md` (private). By now, expect 1-3 Tier A offers OR 1-2 Tier B offers + 1 pending Tier A. For each offer record: firm-tier label (A/B, never the firm name in plaintext), base, sign-on, year-1 RSU/bonus, total year-1 comp, equity vesting cliff/schedule, start-date window, remote/relocation terms, work-authorization mechanism (employer-sponsored vs. self), and verbal-vs-written status. (~30 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: every offer in the inventory has all 9 fields populated or an explicit `[PENDING — chasing]` tag; `grep -c '^## Offer' notes/W131_offers_inventory.md` equals the number of live offers; no field left blank-without-tag.
  - *Mental model*: you cannot negotiate or score what you haven't written down completely — a half-captured offer ("I think the RSU was four-year?") becomes a goal-post you move later under pressure. Capturing all 9 fields now, while the recruiter conversations are fresh, freezes the facts before emotion and deadline anxiety distort them. Written status (verbal vs. paper) matters: a verbal Tier A is leverage, not a decision input.
- [ ] Commit + log (offer details private; only tier-level sums committed). (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Monday inventory commit; the committed file contains tier labels and sums, no plaintext firm names or personally-identifying offer specifics.

**Tuesday — Negotiate**

- [ ] Artifact: `notes/W131_negotiation_log.md` (private). Use competing-offer leverage. Polite, direct, written asks on base, sign-on, and year-1 RSU/bonus — one ask per lever, anchored to a concrete competing number where you hold one. Log each: lever, current number, asked number, justification used, channel (email/call), date, and response. Never accept on the spot; every counter goes back to the inventory. (~30 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: ≥1 written ask sent per top-tier offer; each logged ask names the specific leverage anchor (competing offer tier, market band from levels.fyi, or scarce-skill justification) — no unanchored "can you do better?" asks; the log records the response (or `[awaiting]`) for each.
  - *Mental model*: the offer in hand is a floor, not a ceiling — firms expect a counter and routinely hold 10-20% in reserve, so not asking leaves measurable money on the table for the entire vesting period. Anchoring each ask to a concrete competing number or market band converts the ask from "greedy" to "informed," which is the framing that gets a yes. Asking in writing creates a paper trail and removes the on-call pressure to commit before sleeping on it.
- [ ] Commit + log. (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Tuesday negotiation-log commit.

**Wednesday — Compare against pre-written criteria**

- [ ] Artifact: `notes/W131_scorecard.md` (private). Re-read `notes/W130_offer_criteria.md` and score each offer against THAT rubric only — comp, technical-growth/HFT-relevance, team/manager signal, work-authorization tractability, remote/relocation fit, runtime-hours-to-mastery trajectory. Use the W130 weights; do not invent new criteria or re-weight to favor a frontrunner. Record per-offer scores in a table + a one-line rationale per cell. (~45 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: the scorecard reuses the EXACT criteria set and weights from `notes/W130_offer_criteria.md` (diff the criteria list — it must match; any addition is flagged as `[NEW CRITERION — goal-post check]` with a written justification); every offer scored on every criterion; a single ranked total emerges per offer.
  - *Mental model*: pre-committing the criteria in W130 — before the offers arrived — was the whole point: it inoculates against post-hoc rationalization, where the brain quietly re-weights toward whichever offer feels exciting today. Scoring strictly against the frozen rubric is the discipline that makes Friday's decision defensible to your six-months-from-now self. If a new criterion genuinely surfaces, the goal-post-check tag forces you to justify it in the open rather than smuggle it in.
- [ ] Commit + log. (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Wednesday scorecard commit; the scorecard's criteria list matches W130's.

**Thursday — Sleep on it**

- [ ] Artifact: append a `## Thursday gut-check` section to `notes/W131_scorecard.md` (private). One night minimum between scoring and acceptance — no same-day yes. Record: does the rubric-winner also pass the overnight gut-check? If gut and scorecard disagree, write WHY in one paragraph (the gut is data — it often encodes a manager red-flag or a relocation cost the rubric under-weighted) and decide whether that justifies a rubric revisit or is just cold feet. (~30 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: the gut-check section exists and explicitly states either "gut agrees with rubric-winner" or names the specific divergence + its resolution; the commit timestamp is ≥1 calendar day after the Wednesday scorecard commit (`git log` proves the night actually passed — no compressed same-day decision).
  - *Mental model*: high-stakes irreversible decisions made same-day are biased by recency and the relief of being done; a forced overnight gap lets the slow, integrative parts of judgment surface signals the spreadsheet missed (a dismissive interviewer, a vesting cliff that traps you, a relocation your family can't sustain). The gap is the safeguard against a decision you'd regret by W135. The git-timestamp check makes the "slept on it" claim auditable rather than aspirational.
- [ ] Commit + log. (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Thursday gut-check commit, dated ≥1 day after Wednesday's.

**Friday — Decision**

- [ ] Artifact: `notes/W131_decision.md` (private). Accept ONE offer. Send the acceptance email to the team (confirm start date + work-authorization next steps in writing). Decline the others gracefully and in writing — thank the team, keep the door open, never disparage. Record: which tier accepted, the deciding factor (cite the scorecard rationale), the negotiated start date (the W132 resignation clock keys off this), and confirmation that each declined firm received a courteous written decline. (~30 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: exactly one acceptance recorded; the decision names the deciding criterion by reference to the W131 scorecard (not a fresh rationale invented today); every other live offer has a logged graceful decline; the accepted start date is captured (it is W132's input); offer specifics stay out of the committed plaintext.
  - *Mental model*: declining gracefully is not politeness theater — the crypto-infra / HFT world is small, the engineers you decline today review your PRs and refer you tomorrow, and a clean decline preserves the warm-network capital built over 130 weeks. Anchoring the deciding factor to the scorecard (rather than a Friday gut-feeling) closes the loop: the decision is the rubric's output, which is exactly why you built the rubric. Capturing the start date here is load-bearing — W132's 30-day notice and relocation timeline are computed backward from it.
- [ ] Commit + log (offer details remain private). (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Friday decision commit; committed file records tier + deciding criterion + start date, no plaintext firm identity.

**Saturday — Day-job notice prep (notice submitted W132)**

- [ ] Artifact: `notes/W131_resignation_draft.md` (private). Draft (do NOT send — W132 submits it) the formal 30-day notice to your current employer + a transition-handoff outline: in-flight work, owners to hand to, doc/runbook links, and an offer of transition help. Confirm the last-day date is consistent with the accepted offer's start date (no gap that strands you, no overlap that breaks the notice). (~30 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: the draft states a concrete last-day date that is arithmetically consistent with Friday's negotiated start date (≥30 days notice, no unfunded gap); the handoff outline names ≥1 owner per in-flight item; the file is explicitly marked `DRAFT — submit W132`, so it is not sent prematurely.
  - *Mental model*: the resignation belongs to W132, but drafting it now — while the offer terms and start date are fresh — lets you sanity-check the dates before committing and prevents a panicked, bridge-burning notice next week. "Don't burn bridges; help with transition" is an explicit project constraint (it protects references and the option to return); a calm, prepared handoff is how a 12-year tenure ends as an asset rather than a liability. Last-day arithmetic must reconcile with the start date — a botched gap is a financial and visa-timing hazard.
- [ ] Commit + log. (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Saturday resignation-draft commit; the draft is dated and marked `DRAFT — submit W132`.
