This is a career-integration week, not a Build week. There is no crate, no upstream mirror, and no engineered trap-test arc — manufacturing one would be padding. The runtime-hours milestone (W138: 3800+) advances passively via your already-deployed HFT crates, so the only *code* item this week is a 30-minute maintenance health-check (Saturday) that proves the deployment is still alive, not new development. Every other item is integration work: converting "arrived" into "landed." Each item below carries an explicit private artifact file + an Output line that proves it done; the two reflection/learning items (Tuesday review-etiquette, Friday calibration) carry a hardened-acceptance pass criterion and a mental model of why the item exists in week one and not later. Days are deliberately front- and back-loaded light (~3-4h/day): cultural integration is wall-clock-paced, not effort-paced — you cannot speedrun trust, and over-scheduling week one is itself a cultural misread.

**Monday — Team lunch + relationship mapping**

- [ ] Artifact: `onboarding/team_map.md` (private). Build relationships across the team — accept/initiate a team lunch or coffee, and turn it into a recorded map, not just a pleasant hour. For each teammate you talk to, log: name, role/ownership area, what they're currently working on, and one personal anchor (so the next conversation isn't cold). Mark who owns the systems nearest your future work. (~60 min)
  - *Mental model*: in your first month your single most valuable asset is a mental map of who-owns-what and who-to-ask — it is the difference between being blocked for a day and being unblocked in a Slack message. Writing it down (vs. trusting memory under onboarding overload) is the same discipline as the W120 outreach logs: a concrete artifact beats a vibe. The personal anchor is not small talk; it is the seed of the Depth-3 relationships that, two years out, become the internal-referral channel for *your next* move.
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: `team_map.md` lists ≥3 teammates, each with {name, ownership area, current work, personal anchor}; at least one is tagged `[owns-adjacent]` (owns a system bordering your future scope).
  - Output: `onboarding/team_map.md` exists; `grep -c '\[owns-adjacent\]' onboarding/team_map.md` ≥ 1.

**Tuesday — Code review etiquette (study, don't perform)**

- [ ] Artifact: `onboarding/review_culture.md` (private). Read 3-5 recently-merged PRs in the team's most-active repo and watch how senior folks review. Capture the *norms*, not the code: comment tone (blocking vs. nit vs. question), granularity (line-level vs. design-level), how disagreement is resolved, what gets auto-approved vs. heavily scrutinized, who the de-facto gatekeepers are, and the SLA expectation for turning a review around. (~30 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: `review_culture.md` answers ≥4 of the 6 norm questions {tone, granularity, disagreement-resolution, scrutiny threshold, gatekeepers, review SLA}, each backed by a specific PR link or quoted comment — no generic platitudes ("they're nice"), only observed behavior with evidence.
  - *Mental model*: every firm has an unwritten review dialect, and the fastest way to look like you've-always-been-here (vs. fresh transplant) is to match it before you submit your first PR — not after you've been corrected on it. Studying merged PRs is a read-only, zero-risk way to reverse-engineer the dialect; performing your own style and getting style-corrected in week one is the avoidable version. This is the same "observe the contract before implementing against it" reflex the Build weeks drill, applied to social code.
  - Output: `onboarding/review_culture.md` exists meeting the hardened-acceptance criteria.
- [ ] Commit notes (private). (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Tuesday review-culture commit.

**Wednesday — First small contribution (ramp via a real PR)**

- [ ] Artifact: `onboarding/first_pr.md` (private log) + one real PR in the firm's tracker. Pick the smallest genuinely-useful task you can fully own — a doc fix, a flaky-test stabilization, a small bug, a starter-issue — and open a PR for it, deliberately matching the review dialect captured Tuesday. The goal is not impact; it is to exercise the full local→CI→review→merge loop once while the stakes are tiny. Log: the task, the PR link, and which Tuesday-norm you consciously applied. (~120 min)
  - *Mental model*: the first PR's purpose is to de-risk the *pipeline*, not to impress — you want to discover the CI quirks, the required reviewers, the merge-queue rules, and the local-build gotchas on a change small enough that a mistake costs nothing. Engineers who wait for the "right big task" to make their first contribution stay unintegrated for weeks; shipping something tiny in week one converts you from observer to participant and gives the team a concrete reason to start trusting your judgment.
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: a PR is open (or merged) in the firm's tracker; `first_pr.md` names the task + PR link + the one Tuesday-norm consciously applied (e.g. "used a `question:` prefix per their non-blocking convention").
  - Output: `onboarding/first_pr.md` exists with a PR link; the PR is visible in the firm's tracker.
- [ ] Commit notes (private). (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Wednesday first-PR commit.

**Thursday — Continue ramp: architecture orientation**

- [ ] Artifact: `onboarding/system_map.md` (private). Spend the session orienting to ONE production system you'll touch soon: read its README/design doc, sketch its data flow (inputs → core transform → outputs), and write down the 3 things you don't yet understand as explicit open questions to ask their owner (from Monday's `[owns-adjacent]` tag). Do not try to map the whole codebase — depth on one system beats breadth on ten. (~90 min)
  - *Mental model*: an HFT/infra codebase is too large to absorb by osmosis; the productive move is to pick the system nearest your future work and go deep enough to ask *good* questions of its owner — a precise "I see X feeds the matching path but I can't find where back-pressure is applied" earns respect, whereas "can you explain the whole thing?" signals you haven't done the homework. Writing the open questions down (rather than accumulating vague confusion) turns Friday/next-week conversations into targeted unblocks.
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: `system_map.md` contains a data-flow sketch (inputs/transform/outputs) for ONE named system plus exactly 3 specific open questions, each addressed to a named owner from `team_map.md`.
  - Output: `onboarding/system_map.md` exists with the data-flow sketch + 3 owner-addressed open questions.
- [ ] Commit notes (private). (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Thursday system-map commit.

**Friday — Reflection: how does the new firm differ from prior employers?**

- [ ] Artifact: `onboarding/calibration.md` (private). Calibrate expectations: note what's *better*, what's *different* (explicitly framed as different, not worse — the week-one instinct to read difference as deficiency is the trap). Compare against the baseline you set in W120 and your 12 years of prior employers along concrete axes: decision speed, on-call/incident culture, code-quality bar, autonomy vs. process, communication channels, and pace expectations. (~30 min)
  - 🎯 Hardened acceptance: `calibration.md` covers ≥4 axes; every "different" entry is phrased neutrally with a concrete observation behind it (not "worse" / "annoying"); at least one entry explicitly flags a prior assumption that turned out wrong, so the calibration is real and not confirmation of priors.
  - *Mental model*: the most common way a senior transplant self-sabotages in month one is importing the prior firm's norms as universal truths and silently judging the new firm against them ("at my old shop we'd never ship this"). Forcing yourself to log differences as *neutral* — and to catch at least one wrong prior assumption — rewires that reflex into curiosity, which is what actually lets you absorb the new firm's strengths instead of resisting them. This is the human version of the Build weeks' "predict, then check reality, then correct the drift" loop.
  - Output: `onboarding/calibration.md` exists meeting the hardened-acceptance criteria.
- [ ] Commit notes (private). (~10 min)
  - Output: `git log -1 --oneline` shows the Friday calibration commit.
