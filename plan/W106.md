    claim from runs of THIS binary.

  #### Phase 1 — Problem, requirements, design (~45 min, no code yet)

  - **The problem this primitive solves**: a "feed" that emits the same message at a constant rate gives
    your matching engine no realistic stress. Real ITCH has Zipfian symbol popularity (top symbol = 10%
    of all messages), Pareto inter-arrival (bursty), referential constraints (Cancel must reference an
    Added-but-not-Deleted oid), and occasional storms (market open). A synthetic feed that fails any of
    these masks bugs in the order-book code that would fire in production within seconds. The feed is
    also the entire basis of the M30 uptime audit — without determinism (same seed → same bytes), you
    can't reproduce a bug; without monotonic timestamps, you can't claim 2000 hours of uptime.

  - **Requirements** — write in `crates/marketdata-kernelbypass/notes/106_synthetic_feed_design.md`:
    - R1. Deterministic for a fixed `seed: u64` — two runs produce byte-identical message streams
      (verified by SHA-256 of the first 10 M messages).
    - R2. Timestamps `ts_ns` are monotonic-non-decreasing across the entire run.
    - R3. `oid` is globally unique within a run (per-symbol counter + symbol prefix).
    - R4. Referential closed-world: every Cancel/Execute/Delete/Replace references an `oid` that was
      previously Added and not yet Deleted. A smoke check at end of run asserts no orphan references.
    - R5. Symbol popularity is Zipf(α=1.1); top symbol gets ~10% of all messages, top 10 get ~50%.
    - R6. Message mix matches NASDAQ published ratios — 60% AddOrder, 15% Cancel, 15% Execute, 5%
      Replace, 5% Delete (tunable via config).
    - R7. Rate shaping: sustains `rate_target` msg/sec to within ±2% measured over 10 s windows.
      Uses busy-wait below 1 ms (no `tokio::time::sleep` on the hot path).
    - R8. Storm injection: every ~60 s, emit a 100k-msg burst over 100 ms; `storm_count_total` metric
      tracks them.
    - R9. Backpressure: when downstream consumer can't keep up, the feed slows down (queue blocks) —
      it never buffer-bloats unbounded.
    - R10. Sustains 1 M msg/sec for ≥ 60 minutes with all invariants holding.

  - **Design walk-through** — answer each in your design doc; no peeking at `arctic-stream/nasdaq-itch`
    until Phase 3:
    - D1. RNG choice: `rand::thread_rng()` vs `rand_xoshiro::Xoshiro256PlusPlus` vs `rand_pcg::Pcg64`.
      Argue from R1 — which is deterministic across thread counts? Why thread_rng is forbidden.
    - D2. Order-book shadow: data structure to track per-symbol open `oid`s for R4. `HashMap<SymId,
      Vec<oid>>` vs `IntervalSet` vs `RoaringBitmap`. Which has acceptable Cancel-reference cost at
      1 M msg/sec?
    - D3. Inter-arrival sampling: Pareto distribution. Where does it sit relative to rate shaping?
      (Hint: Pareto is per-message jitter; rate target is integrated over 10 s windows.)
    - D4. Rate-shaping implementation: `spin_sleep` vs `std::thread::sleep` vs busy-wait + monotonic
      clock. Argue from R7 — sub-ms accuracy requires what? What does `tokio::time::sleep`'s ~1 ms
      floor on Linux cost you?
    - D5. Storm injection: how do you generate 100k messages in 100 ms without violating R4 (referential
      closed world)? (Hint: pre-allocate `oid`s for the storm; emit Add+Cancel pairs to maintain
      closed-world.)
    - D6. Backpressure: `BumpyBoundedQueue<ItchMessage>` vs `crossbeam::channel::bounded` vs
      `tokio::sync::mpsc::channel`. Which gives you the right semantics for R9? Trade-offs.
    - D7. Symbol universe sizing: how many symbols? Why 1024 not 100 or 10_000? Reference NASDAQ symbol
      count + how Zipf tails matter at the long end.
    - D8. **Pre-mortem (the load-bearing question)**: write the 6-step interleaving where a Cancel for
      oid=42 is emitted before the Add for oid=42 because the storm-injection path bypassed the
      order-book shadow. Convince yourself R4's invariant check catches it BEFORE the M30 audit.
  - **Output**: `notes/106_synthetic_feed_design.md` with R1–R10, answers to D1–D8, the message-mix
    table with NASDAQ citations, the storm-injection sequence diagram.

  #### Phase 3 — Make the tests pass, one failure at a time (~75 min)

  - **When `seed_determinism_byte_identical` reports hashes differ between runs:**
    - Symptom: same seed=42, two runs, different SHA-256.
    - Why: code used `rand::thread_rng()` somewhere (e.g. jitter sampling, storm trigger) which is
      seeded from OS entropy on first use per thread.
    - Fix: ALL RNGs derive from a single per-run `Xoshiro256PlusPlus::seed_from_u64(seed)`. Sub-streams
      via `rng.split()` for independent generators (feed, jitter, storm). NEVER call `thread_rng()`.
      Lint: a `cargo clippy` deny rule on `thread_rng`.
    - Muscle: determinism = one seed source, no implicit OS entropy; thread_rng is forbidden in
      reproducible simulators. Reapplies at: W107 ops-chaos seed handling, W109 VOPR scenario replay.

  - **When `timestamps_monotonic_nondecreasing` finds a non-monotonic ts at position 8742:**
    - Symptom: `ts_ns[8742] < ts_ns[8741]`; the consumer rejects the message.
    - Why: rate-shaper used `std::time::Instant::now()` which is NOT guaranteed monotonic across CPU
      migrations on older kernels. OR: storm-injection used a separate clock that didn't advance the
      shared cursor.
    - Fix: feed maintains `clock: SyntheticClock` (a `AtomicU64<ts_ns>`) that ONLY moves forward via
      `clock.advance(delta_ns)`. All message timestamps read from this clock; rate-shaping computes
      `delta_ns` from the Pareto sample and advances exactly once per message.
    - Muscle: monotonic time in simulation = synthetic clock, not wall clock; wall clock is a hardware
      detail you should not depend on. Reapplies at: W107 ops-chaos test clock, W109 VOPR clock model.

  - **When `closed_world_referential_check` finds 142 Cancels referencing never-Added oids:**
    - Symptom: 142 orphan references; all happen during storm bursts.
    - Why: storm-injection path generated Cancel messages directly from the RNG without first checking
      the order-book shadow; storm assumed any oid was valid.
    - Fix: storm-injection pre-allocates a window of oids, emits Adds for all of them FIRST, then
      emits Cancels/Executes referencing only those. The order-book shadow is updated for every emitted
      message (Add inserts, Delete removes). Cancel/Execute/Replace pick from currently-open oids.
    - Muscle: closed-world invariants in synthetic data require a "shadow" model that tracks open
      state; storms must respect the model. Reapplies at: W107 ops-chaos workload generation,
      W109 VOPR scenario invariants.

  - **When `rate_shaping_within_2pct` reports actual rate = 91k vs target 100k:**
    - Symptom: 9% rate undershoot; profile shows the feed thread sleeps in `tokio::time::sleep` calls.
    - Why: rate-shaping used `tokio::time::sleep(Duration::from_micros(10))` which on Linux floors at
      ~1 ms (timer wheel resolution), losing 100× the intended pacing.
    - Fix: use `spin_sleep::sleep` for sub-ms (busy-waits the last <1 ms). For ≥1 ms, `std::thread::sleep`
      is fine. NEVER `tokio::time::sleep` on the feed hot path.
    - Cost avoided: 9% throughput shortfall; at 1 M msg/sec target, that's 90k msg/sec lost — the M30
      "1 M msg/sec sustained" claim falls apart.
    - Muscle: tokio's timer wheel is for human-scale (≥1 ms); HFT-scale pacing needs spin or
      `clock_nanosleep`. Reapplies at: matching-engine internal pacing, W107 chaos timing.

  - **When `backpressure_no_unbounded_buffer` reports queue depth = 50 M after 5 s pause:**
    - Symptom: pause consumer for 5 s; queue grows from 1k → 50 M entries, OOM.
    - Why: queue was `crossbeam::channel::unbounded`; producer never blocks.
    - Fix: queue is `BumpyBoundedQueue` with capacity 100k. Producer's `push` returns `Err(Full)`; feed
      handles by sleeping a short jitter and re-attempting. Rate shaper sees the backpressure as
      slowed-down inter-arrival and the rate naturally drops to consumer speed.
    - Muscle: any producer-consumer pipeline needs bounded queue + producer-side handling of Full;
      unbounded queues are OOM bombs. Reapplies at: W105 tracing bridge queue, W107 chaos queue.

  - **When `storm_injection_fires_periodically` reports 0 storms in 5 minutes:**
    - Symptom: `storm_count_total` is 0; no storms fired.
    - Why: storm trigger used a wall clock that didn't advance in the simulated clock (or used
      `Instant::now()` which the test's mock clock didn't drive).
    - Fix: storm trigger derives its next-storm-at from the SyntheticClock; trigger fires when
      `clock.now() >= next_storm_at`. Independent of wall clock.
    - Muscle: tests of timed events need the synthetic clock to drive triggers — never the wall clock.
      Reapplies at: W107 chaos timing tests, W109 VOPR scheduled events.

  - **5-year failure mode** (write in `notes/106_synthetic_feed_followup.md` after green): when the feed
    needs to model L3 order-book reconstruction with full participant identity (post-MiFID II / RegSCI
    requirements), the Zipf-only symbol model is insufficient — participant flow has its own Zipf and

    need (LSE/NASDAQ TotalView vs synthetic) and the new invariant (per-participant rate caps).
