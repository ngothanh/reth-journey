- [ ] **Concurrency drill #17 of 28 — `RwLock<T>` from scratch (atomic-count protocol)** (solo, ~6 hrs).
  Build a reader-writer lock using the canonical signed-int trick: +N = N readers, -1 = a writer, 0 = free.
  Workload: many concurrent readers + occasional writer; assert mutual exclusion of writer vs anyone.
  Build `crates/concurrent/src/rwlock.rs` — `RwLock<T> { count: AtomicI64, data: UnsafeCell<T> }` with
  `read(&self) -> ReadGuard<'_, T>` and `write(&self) -> WriteGuard<'_, T>`. Spin on contention; no parking.
  Constraints: no peek at `std::sync::RwLock`, `parking_lot::RwLock`, or `crossbeam::sync::ShardedLock`
  until COMPARE.
  - **Spec**: invariants for (a) at most one writer; (b) when writer holds, no readers; (c) writer is
    exclusive of reads. Predict the CAS protocol for each: read-acquire CASs `count, count+1` when
    `count >= 0`; write-acquire CASs `0, -1`. Then predict the orderings — what synchronizes-with what?
  - **Build**: Relaxed everywhere on the CAS. Smoke single-thread (1 writer can write; readers see).
  - **Loom**: 2 readers + 1 writer test. Asserts no torn read during writer's mutation (writer flips byte
    `0xAA` to `0xBB` on a shared buffer; reader observes either all-AA or all-BB, never mixed). Iterate
    ordering ONE at a time; max 3 attempts.
  - **Compare**: open `parking_lot::RwLock` source. They have multiple lock-state bits encoded in one
    AtomicUsize (reader count, writer flag, waiter bits). What does the extra waiter-bit machinery enable
    that yours doesn't? Then open `std::sync::RwLock` — it delegates to OS primitives — why is that fine
    for std but wrong for HFT?
  - **Symptom card**: bug shape (writer's mutation observed mid-flight by reader because read-acquire
    didn't Acquire); rule (read-acquire's CAS must Acquire; write-release's CAS must Release; writer
    establishes the synchronization edge for ALL readers); reapplies at: W42 LockManager shared/exclusive
    locks, every storage-trie reader/writer separation.
  - **Numbers**: predict read-acquire latency at low contention (~10 ns CAS); write-acquire under reader
    convoy (potentially unbounded — that's drill #18). Predict N=8 readers + 1 writer aggregate read
    throughput (writer rarely succeeds — that's the convoy bug).
  - **5-year failure mode**: at very high reader concurrency, the read counter itself becomes the
    contention point (every reader CASs it). Trigger: 64+ reader threads. Fix: sharded reader count
    (per-NUMA shards, drill #14 pattern).
  - **Production handoff**: this RwLock primitive lands at W42 LockManager (shared = read, exclusive =
    write). W42 hardened acceptance forces you to discover convoy via load test — drill #18 next is the
    fix.

**Wednesday — ME ch5-6 + `Block` + `Body` + concurrency drill #18**

- [ ] **Concurrency drill #18 of 28 — writer starvation under reader convoy + a fairness fix** (solo, ~5 hrs).
  Drill #17's RwLock is unfair: under a steady stream of readers, the writer waits forever. Show this
  empirically, then derive a fix.
  Extend drill #17's `RwLock` with a fairness mode. Workload: 8 readers continuously taking the lock, 1
  writer trying. Measure writer's wait time under (a) naive, (b) "writer-priority" mode where once a
  writer is waiting, no new readers may acquire.
  Constraints: no peek at `parking_lot::FairRwLock`, no peek at Rust's `RwLock::Fairness` mode until COMPARE.
  - **Spec**: predict the wait-time distribution for naive (likely: writer never proceeds; p99 = infinity).
    Predict the throughput impact of writer-priority mode on the readers (some convoy slowdown — measure
    the trade-off).
  - **Build**: extend drill #17 with a `writer_waiting: AtomicBool` flag. Reader's CAS checks both
    `count >= 0` AND `!writer_waiting`. Writer sets the flag before its acquire-CAS spin. Measure both
    modes.
  - **Loom**: not the tool for fairness — fairness is a probabilistic property under uniform schedulers.
    Use criterion or a custom microbench loop with N readers + 1 writer for 10s, count writer acquisitions.
  - **Compare**: open `parking_lot::FairRwLock` source. They use an explicit FIFO queue of waiters — what
    additional bookkeeping (memory + cache traffic) does that add over your single-flag approach?
  - **Symptom card**: bug shape (writer starvation = unbounded wait, p99 = infinity); rule (any priority
    inversion needs an explicit fairness mechanism — flag or queue; the cheaper flag is right when writes
    are rare); reapplies at: W42 LockManager wound-wait older-vs-younger priority, W74 raft leader-vs-
    follower election fairness.
  - **Numbers**: predict writer wait under 8 readers steady (naive: ∞; writer-priority: ~10 ns per reader
    that finishes its current CS). Predict reader throughput cost of fairness (~5-10% — readers that
    arrive AFTER writer-waiting block briefly).
  - **5-year failure mode**: in long-running production, the rare-but-important writer can starve under
    workload-pattern shifts (read burst). Trigger: workload mix changes. Fix: enforce fairness from day
    one, not after the incident.
  - **Production handoff**: writer-priority lands at W42 LockManager (wound-wait IS this pattern at age
    granularity), W74 raft leader-elect (a new-leader prevents perpetual re-election by stable-leader
    heuristic). W42 hardened acceptance forbids "writer never proceeds" — fairness gate.
