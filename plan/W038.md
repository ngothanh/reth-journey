  `MemTable { skip_list: concurrent::SkipMap<Key, ValueWithTombstone>, size: AtomicUsize }`. Consumes the lock-free
  concurrent skiplist built in W37 (mirror of `crossbeam-skiplist`, EBR-backed). Skyzh's `Arc<RwLock<BTreeMap>>`
  design from tutorial Day 1 is the baseline you must beat under ≥4 concurrent writers; if your skiplist doesn't
  win at 4 threads, EBR overhead is mistuned (revisit W37 Fri bench). (~total 220 min across 3 phases)
  - 🔧 **Why this matters**: the memtable is the LSM write hot path; every put traverses it. Wrong value-type
    (Vec instead of Bytes) → ~1 GB/sec of malloc traffic at 1 M put/sec. Wrong counter design → 16-thread
    throughput plateau. Downstream W39 flush, W40 STCS compaction, W95 mini-db memtable layer all inherit.

  #### Phase 1 — Problem, requirements, design (~50 min, no code yet)

  - **The problem this primitive solves**: a write-hot sorted in-memory container with tombstone semantics
    that flushes to disk when it crosses a size threshold. The two perf cliffs: per-put allocation (Vec vs
    Bytes) and counter contention (single AtomicUsize vs sharded). The semantic cliff: delete = insert
    Tombstone, NEVER remove.

  - **Requirements** — write in `crates/lsm-core/notes/38_memtable_design.md`:
    - R1. `put(k, v)` inserts `Value(Bytes)` into the SkipMap; `delete(k)` inserts `Tombstone`.
    - R2. **Tombstone semantics**: deletes are inserts of a Tombstone variant; they suppress lower-level reads
      until the tombstone is elided at the deepest compaction level.
    - R3. **Value type is `Bytes` (Arc-backed)** — zero-copy clone; `Value(Vec<u8>)` is forbidden.
    - R4. **Size counter is sharded** — `SizeShards { shards: [CachePadded<AtomicUsize>; 16] }`; thread-id
      hash picks the shard for bumps. Flush threshold check sums all shards lazily.
    - R5. ≥ 1 M put/sec single-thread; ≥ 5 M/sec aggregate at 16 threads (beats Arc<RwLock<BTreeMap>> by
      ≥ 2× at 4 threads).
    - R6. Scan is bounded-cost; long flush scans operate on a frozen (immutable) memtable handoff to avoid
      stalling writers.
    - R7. Zero allocations on put after skiplist node alloc (the one malloc per put from W37).

  - **Design walk-through** — answer each:
    - D1. Why `Bytes` (`bytes::Bytes`, Arc-backed) instead of `Vec<u8>`? Cite the clone cost (~30 ns
      Arc-bump vs malloc+memcpy). What about owned-then-shared (`Bytes::from(vec)`)?
    - D2. Tombstone as enum variant: write the `pub enum ValueOrTombstone { Value(Bytes), Tombstone }`.
      Why is this preferred over `Option<Bytes>` (the tombstone is semantically meaningful, not absence)?
    - D3. Sharded counter design: write the `SizeShards` struct. Why 16 shards? Why CachePadded? How does
      thread-id-hash pick a shard?
    - D4. Flush threshold check: sum the shards. How often is this called? Why is "lazy sum" acceptable?
    - D5. Iterator hold across rotation: a long scan holds an EBR guard. What's the policy when the
      memtable rotates mid-scan?
    - D6. **Pre-mortem (load-bearing)**: write the per-put `Vec` allocation trace at 1 M ops/sec ×1 KB
      values = 1 GB/sec of malloc/free; then the counter-bottleneck trace at 16 threads.
  - **Output**: `notes/38_memtable_design.md` with R1–R7, D1–D6, both traces.

  #### Phase 3 — Make the tests pass, one failure at a time (~120 min)

  - **When `bench_memtable_put_no_per_op_alloc` reports 2 allocations per put:**
    - Symptom: at 1 M put/sec the allocator counter shows 2 M allocs/sec; flamegraph dominated by
      `malloc` and `__memcpy_avx_unaligned`. Throughput plateaus at ~500 k/sec because of allocator
      contention.
    - Why: declaring the value variant as `Value(Vec<u8>)` instead of `Value(Bytes)` means every put
      either copies the caller's buffer or re-allocates from a slice. At 1 M put/sec with 1 KB avg
      value, that's ~1 GB/sec of malloc traffic — the allocator (and downstream heap fragmentation)
      becomes the bottleneck even though the skiplist itself is fast.
    - Fix: `Value(Bytes)` (`bytes::Bytes`, Arc-backed). Zero-copy clone. Callers pass `Bytes` from
      already-shared buffers (Tokio reads, RPC bodies). For owned-then-shared, `Bytes::from(vec)` is
      one move + Arc-bump, ~30 ns. Tombstone variant is a no-payload enum tag — free.
    - Cost avoided: at 1 KB values × 1 M put/sec ≈ 1 GB/sec of malloc + free → ~1-3 seconds of CPU/sec
      recovered + heap fragmentation eliminated.
    - Muscle: value types in hot containers use Arc-backed clones (`Bytes`, `Arc<[u8]>`), not owned
      `Vec<u8>`. Reapplies at: W39 SSTable block value, W42 `txn` write-set, W74 matching-engine order
      payload, W77 aeron frame payload.

  - **When `bench_memtable_16_threads_scales` reports the counter as the bottleneck (16-thread
    throughput ≤ 2× single-thread):**
    - Symptom: 16-thread bench plateaus at ~2 M put/sec (vs expected 5+ M); flamegraph shows the
      `size.fetch_add` line dominating. CachePadded on the single `AtomicUsize` doesn't help.
    - Why: under the LSM write path, the memtable's `size: AtomicUsize` is bumped on every insert. On
      16-thread bench, the atomic counter becomes the bottleneck — CachePadded on `size` doesn't help
      because the contention is *on the counter itself*, not false-sharing.
    - Fix: per-thread size shards; sum lazily when the flusher needs the total. `SizeShards { shards:
      [CachePadded<AtomicUsize>; 16] }`; thread-id hash → shard index; `fetch_add` on the shard. Flush
      check does one O(16) sum (cheap; called rarely).
    - Muscle: counter sharding is the canonical fix for "atomic on a hot path is now the hot path."
      Reapplies at: matching-engine fill-counter (W74), messaging-aeron position publishing (W77).

  - **When `memtable_delete_returns_tombstone_not_none` reports None:**
    - Symptom: after delete(k), get(k) returns None; W39 flush test reports ghost reads (older SSTable
      still serves the key).
    - Why: implementing `delete` as a SkipMap remove loses the suppression signal — older SSTable still
      serves the key. The memtable doesn't "know" that lower levels need suppression.
    - Fix: tombstone is a value variant, never a remove. `delete(k)` calls `skip_list.insert(k,
      Tombstone)`. Flush writes the Tombstone variant; SSTable reader returns it; query layer
      interprets Tombstone as "deleted" and stops the LSM lookup chain.
    - Muscle: LSM tombstones are first-class values, not absence-of-value. Reapplies at: W39 SSTable
      writer, W40 STCS compaction tombstone retention.

  - **5-year failure mode** (write in `notes/38_memtable_followup.md` after green): when persistent
    memory (PMem) becomes mainstream, the memtable can be a persistent log instead of in-memory; flush
    becomes a pointer flip instead of a copy. State the migration: introduce `PMemMemTable` variant
    behind a feature flag; trigger when PMem latency < DRAM latency.

**Tuesday — `lsm-core` merge iterator**

- [ ] **Build**: `crates/lsm-core/src/iterator.rs` — `MergeIterator<I: KvIterator>` k-way merge via a min-heap over (
  key, source_idx, value). Tie-break by source_idx (newer wins). (~total 220 min across 3 phases)
  - 🔧 **Why this matters**: every LSM read crosses N sorted streams (memtable + L0 + per-level SSTs); this
    primitive is the merge engine. Tie-break direction is consensus-critical — wrong direction → deleted keys
    reappear (silent corruption). Downstream W39 SST read path, W40 STCS compaction, W42 txn snapshot reads
    all consume.

  #### Phase 1 — Problem, requirements, design (~50 min, no code yet)

  - **The problem this primitive solves**: merge N sorted streams into one sorted output stream; on equal
    keys across streams, keep the newer (lowest source_idx). The trick is the tie-break direction; getting it
    wrong is silent because the data still flows.

  - **Requirements** — write in `crates/lsm-core/notes/38_iterator_design.md`:
    - R1. `MergeIterator` wraps a min-heap over `HeapEntry { key, source_idx, iter }`.
    - R2. Output is strictly sorted ascending by key.
    - R3. **Tie-break: lower source_idx wins** — source 0 = memtable (newest); higher idx = older levels.
      Equal-key entries with higher idx are advanced past silently.
    - R4. **`Ord` implemented directly** (not via PartialOrd) — both `key` and `source_idx` are total.
    - R5. **Advance-past-equal discipline** — after emitting one entry for key K, every other heap entry
      with key K is advanced and re-pushed.
    - R6. Tombstones flow through — caller is responsible for suppression downstream.
    - R7. ≥ 50 M output/sec on warm 4-way merge; ≥ 20 M/sec on 10-way merge.
    - R8. Zero allocations after initial heap construction.

  - **Design walk-through** — answer each:
    - D1. Why a min-heap over a custom tournament tree? When N = 4 they're equivalent; when N = 100?
    - D2. Write the `Ord` impl: `cmp` order by `key.cmp(other.key)` then `source_idx.cmp(other.source_idx)`.
      Why this order (not the reverse)?
    - D3. Tie-break direction trace: insert (k=5, idx=0, val=Tombstone) and (k=5, idx=1, val=V). Which
      emits? What if the tie-break is reversed?
    - D4. Seek protocol: each inner iter calls `seek(target)`; some land valid, some exhausted. Why must
      you filter `is_valid()` BEFORE pushing to heap?
    - D5. `next()` algorithm: write the 5-step pseudocode. What's the invariant after each step?
    - D6. **Pre-mortem (load-bearing)**: write the tie-break-wrong trace where tombstone from source 0 is
      shadowed by a value from source 2 — the deleted key reappears in the output stream.
  - **Output**: `notes/38_iterator_design.md` with R1–R8, D1–D6.

  #### Phase 3 — Make the tests pass, one failure at a time (~120 min)

  - **When `merge_tombstone_from_newer_source_wins` reports the Value (older) winning:**
    - Symptom: source 0 has Tombstone for k=5; source 1 has Value(v); output emits Value(v). The
      "deleted" key reappears.
    - Why: tie-break direction wrong. If source 0 is oldest (not newest), tombstones don't suppress
      and stale data wins. Deleted keys reappear.
    - Fix: document and enforce — source 0 = memtable (newest); higher idx = older levels. In `Ord`
      impl, equal-key tie-break is `source_idx.cmp(other.source_idx)` (lower idx = "less" = pops
      first from min-heap = winner).
    - Muscle: LSM merge tie-break is consensus-critical; document the convention at the trait level
      and enforce in tests. Reapplies at: W40 STCS compaction, W42 txn snapshot reads, W95 mini-db
      query path.

  - **When `merge_advance_past_equal_no_duplicates` reports duplicate emissions:**
    - Symptom: output contains k=5 twice; the second emission comes from a heap entry that wasn't
      advanced past the equal key.
    - Why: leaving non-kept equal-key entries in the heap will re-emit them on next `pop`.
    - Fix: on equal key, advance the inner iterator and re-push to heap. After emitting the winner
      (lowest idx), pop every other entry with same key, call `next()` on its iter, re-push (or
      discard if exhausted).
    - Muscle: heap-based merge must advance ALL equal-key entries past the emitted key, not just the
      winner. Reapplies at: W40 STCS compaction tombstone elision, W101 vector-db candidate-list
      merge.

  - **When `merge_seek_filters_exhausted_iters` reports a panic during `next()`:**
    - Symptom: after seek, some iters are exhausted; you push them anyway; heap pops them; `key()` on
      an exhausted iter panics.
    - Why: iterator validity check after seek — post-seek some iterators are exhausted; pushing
      exhausted ones into heap will pop garbage.
    - Fix: filter `is_valid()` before push. After every `seek()` call on an inner iter, gate the heap
      push on `iter.is_valid()`.
    - Muscle: any heap of iterators must validate-then-push; exhausted iters never enter the heap.
      Reapplies at: W40 STCS compaction, W101 vector-db sealed-segment merge.

  - **When `merge_ord_not_partialord` fails to compile / `merge_two_streams_sorted_output` reports
    missing rows:**
    - Symptom: random rows missing from output; `HeapEntry` uses `PartialOrd` derive; on some pairs,
      `partial_cmp` returns `None` and heap behavior is undefined.
    - Why: PartialOrd allows None comparisons → heap behavior undefined. Missing rows in output.
    - Fix: implement `Ord` directly; `key` and `source_idx` are both total. `impl Ord for HeapEntry {
      fn cmp(&self, other: &Self) -> Ordering { self.key.cmp(&other.key).then(self.source_idx.cmp(&
      other.source_idx)) } }`.
    - Muscle: heap entries MUST implement `Ord`, not `PartialOrd`. Reapplies at: every priority queue,
      W40 compaction picker.

  - **5-year failure mode** (write in `notes/38_iterator_followup.md` after green): when SIMD
    multi-way merge primitives stabilize (e.g. AVX-512 sort+merge), the binary-heap design can be
    replaced with a tournament tree + vector compare. State the migration: gate via target-cpu; scalar
    heap remains portable fallback.

- [ ] **Build**: `crates/lsm-core/src/block.rs` — `Block { offsets: Vec<u16>, data: Bytes, restart_interval: u16 }`.
  Restart-point compression (LevelDB-style). (~total 220 min across 3 phases)
  - 🔧 **Why this matters**: SSTable blocks are the I/O unit of the LSM. Wrong varint width → silent
    truncation. Wrong seek logic on prefix-shared keys → missed reads. Downstream W39 SSTable builder, W40
    compaction reader, W95 mini-db all consume.

  #### Phase 1 — Problem, requirements, design (~50 min, no code yet)

  - **The problem this primitive solves**: pack sorted KV pairs into 4-32 KB blocks with prefix compression
    (each entry stores `(shared, unshared, value, unshared_key, value_bytes)`); periodic "restart points" hold
    full keys to enable binary search. Decode reconstructs the full key by combining shared bytes from the
    prior key with unshared bytes from the current entry.

  - **Requirements** — write in `crates/lsm-core/notes/38_block_design.md`:
    - R1. Entries in a block are key-sorted; binary search on restart points + linear scan within group.
    - R2. Restart point every `restart_interval` entries (default 16); restart entries have shared=0.
    - R3. Entry encoding: `varint(shared) || varint(unshared) || varint(value_len) || unshared_key ||
      value`.
    - R4. **u32 varints** throughout — keys >65 KB must NOT silently truncate.
    - R5. **Seek operates on restart-point full keys** — never compare against prefix-elided keys; linear
      scan within restart group reconstructs full keys.
    - R6. Block trailer: `restarts.len(): u32 + restarts: [u32; N]`.
    - R7. ≥ 100 k entries/sec encode; ≥ 1 M entries/sec decode (iter).
    - R8. Compression ratio ≥ 3× for sorted keys with shared prefixes.

  - **Design walk-through** — answer each:
    - D1. Why varint instead of fixed u32 for the three lengths? When does each entry's overhead drop?
    - D2. Restart interval trade-off: 1 = no compression (every entry full); 64 = expensive intra-group
      seek (~30 entries linear scan). Why 16?
    - D3. Decoder iterator: write the pseudocode. What state must it maintain to reconstruct keys
      (hint: `last_key: BytesMut`)?
    - D4. Seek within block: write the algorithm. What does the binary search compare? What does the
      linear scan compare?
    - D5. Block trailer layout: why store `restarts.len()` separately from the array? How does the
      reader find it?
    - D6. **Pre-mortem (load-bearing)**: write the u16-varint-overflow trace — a key of 66 KB encodes
      with truncated length; decoder reads `66 KB & 0xFFFF` = ~512 bytes; the rest of the block is
      misaligned garbage. Then write the seek-on-shared-prefix trace where binary search compares a
      partial key against a full target.
  - **Output**: `notes/38_block_design.md` with R1–R8, D1–D6.

  #### Phase 3 — Make the tests pass, one failure at a time (~120 min)

  - **When `block_varint_handles_large_keys` reports the decoder reading a key of ~512 bytes (instead
    of 100 KB):**
    - Symptom: encode a 100 KB key; decoder reads back ~512 bytes; subsequent entries are misaligned
      garbage. Block effectively corrupted.
    - Why: keys >65 KB break u16-based varint. Silent truncation — encoder writes `key.len() &
      0xFFFF` and the decoder reads that truncated length. No error; the remaining bytes are read as
      payload, misaligning the entire block.
    - Fix: u32 varints throughout, or assert max key size. Prefer u32 varints — they handle up to
      ~256 MB / entry, which is well beyond realistic key sizes. Encoding cost adds ~1 byte/entry
      for sub-128-byte values; negligible.
    - Muscle: choosing varint width = choosing the silent-truncation threshold; always use u32 unless
      proven otherwise. Reapplies at: W39 SSTable footer offsets, W77 aeron header field widths.

  - **When `block_seek_random_keys` reports missed reads on prefix-shared keys:**
    - Symptom: seek for "user_id_12345" returns the wrong entry; binary search comparison was against
      a partially-decoded key whose shared prefix was elided.
    - Why: seek correctness on prefix-shared keys — binary search comparing partially decoded keys
      (with prefix elided) is wrong. The binary search compares "12345" (the unshared part) against
      "user_id_12345" (the full target).
    - Fix: search operates on restart-point full keys; linear scan with full reconstruction within
      group. Binary search compares full keys (at restart points, where `shared=0`); intra-group
      linear scan reconstructs each key as `last_key[..shared] || unshared`.
    - Muscle: any compressed representation makes seek operate on the uncompressed (restart) view,
      not the compressed entries. Reapplies at: W39 SSTable block index seek, W101 vector-db
      sealed-segment posting list.

  - **When `bench_block_encode_100k_under_50ms` reports ~150 ms instead of ~50 ms:**
    - Symptom: encode of 100 k entries takes 3× the target; flamegraph shows `BytesMut::extend`
      reallocating multiple times.
    - Why: BytesMut growth policy is doubling; for a 16 KB block with 1 K entries averaging 16 bytes
      each, growth fires ~9 times (16 → 32 → 64 → ... → 16384), each grow = malloc + memcpy + free.
    - Fix: pre-size `BytesMut::with_capacity(target_block_size)` (e.g. 16 KB). Compute the size from
      the configured target.
    - Muscle: BytesMut growth is amortized O(1) but each grow is slow; pre-size when the target is
      known. Reapplies at: W4 BytesMut for RLP encoding, W77 aeron frame builder.

  - **5-year failure mode** (write in `notes/38_block_followup.md` after green): when columnar
    compressed formats (Parquet-style) become standard in LSM (research-grade as of 2025), the
    restart-point layout is replaced by per-column dictionaries + RLE. State the migration: introduce
    `ColumnarBlock` variant; trigger when bloom-filter cost dominates seek (perf-shaped, not
    correctness-shaped).
