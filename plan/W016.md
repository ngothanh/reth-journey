- [ ] **Concurrency drill #23 of 28 — EBR pin/unpin race (the W33 build, in miniature)** (solo, ~6 hrs).
  Epoch-based reclamation amortizes hazard pointers: instead of per-pointer protection, threads "pin" an
  epoch; nodes retired in epoch E are reclaimed only after all threads have advanced past E. The race that
  trips students is the unpinned-but-about-to-pin window — thread T is currently unpinned, advance-epoch
  fires, T then re-pins reading a now-freed pointer.
  Build `crates/concurrent/src/ebr_mini.rs` — minimal EBR with `global_epoch: AtomicUsize`, per-thread
  `pin_slot: AtomicUsize` (0 = unpinned, ≥1 = pinned in that epoch), `Guard` with RAII pin/unpin,
  `defer_destroy(p)` push to a per-thread bag indexed by current epoch, `try_advance()` that bumps
  `global_epoch` IFF all per-thread pins are unpinned OR pinned at current epoch.
  Constraints: no peek at `crossbeam_epoch::Collector`, `crossbeam_epoch::Guard`, or the W33 plan content
  until COMPARE.
  - **Spec**: write out the unpinned-but-about-to-pin race in your spec doc. Two threads: A is pinned in
    epoch 5 with a pointer ptr, B does advance-epoch to 6, A unpins, A re-pins in epoch 6, A then derefs
    ptr — but ptr was retired in epoch 5 and freed during the advance. Predict which fence is required to
    close this race. Hint: it's not Acquire/Release — those are insufficient.
  - **Build**: Relaxed everywhere on pin/unpin/advance.
  - **Loom**: 2-thread harness — thread A pins, reads a pointer, unpins, re-pins, derefs (with poison
    check on the pointee). Thread B retires the pointer, advances. Naive must UAF; the fix must not.
    Iterate orderings; max 3 attempts. The answer for the pin op involves SeqCst — derive why.
  - **Compare**: open `crossbeam_epoch::Guard::pin` source. Their use of `fence(SeqCst)` between pin-store
    and epoch-load. The exact same dance you derived. Read the comment they wrote about WHY.
  - **Symptom card**: bug shape (UAF after re-pin in advanced epoch); rule (pin's store and pin's epoch-
    load must be jointly ordered by SeqCst — Release-Acquire allows the store to be reordered with the
    load on weak hardware); reapplies at: W33 (the real EBR build), W37 skiplist using EBR, W42 txn MVCC
    versions.
  - **Numbers**: predict pin/unpin overhead (~10-15 ns per pin including SeqCst fence; vs ~5 ns for
    Acquire-only — the cost of correctness). Predict advance frequency at sustained load (typically
    every ~1k retirements).
  - **5-year failure mode**: pin overhead matters at extreme rates (>50M ops/sec); SeqCst fence becomes
    visible in profile. Trigger: ledger-deterministic at peak. Fix: per-NUMA epoch counters.
  - **Production handoff**: this primitive IS the W33 build at full scale. W33's hardened acceptance
    (when we get there) silently requires the SeqCst pin discipline you just derived. W37 skiplist
    consumes; W42 OCC MVCC consumes.

**Wednesday — Foundry cast + `eth-rpc-types/filter` + concurrency drill #24**

- [ ] **Concurrency drill #24 of 28 — EBR `defer_destroy` + advance ordering (double-free hunt)** (solo, ~5 hrs).
  Drill #23's EBR can still double-free if you mishandle advance: thread A's bag contains ptr retired in
  epoch 5; advance to 6 happens; thread B's bag also contains ptr (because A and B both retired the same
  ptr — buggy higher-level code, but a real failure mode). Or: bag-draining races between threads.
  Extend drill #23's `ebr_mini` with proper bag management: per-thread bags indexed by epoch (3-slot ring,
  current/prior/two-prior), drain happens during advance, drain is exclusive per bag.
  Constraints: no peek at `crossbeam_epoch::Local::flush`, `crossbeam_epoch::Bag` until COMPARE.
  - **Spec**: invariants for (a) each pointer freed exactly once across all bags, (b) bag draining doesn't
    race with concurrent deferred-destroys to the same bag. Predict the failure if two threads concurrently
    push to the same bag without coordination.
  - **Build**: 3-slot bag per thread, indexed by `current_epoch % 3`. Drain bag two-epochs-back during
    advance.
  - **Loom**: 2-thread test: both threads retire pointers, both advance. Asserts no double-free and no
    skipped-free. Iterate orderings; max 3.
  - **Compare**: open `crossbeam_epoch::Local::flush` source. Their bag implementation has a max-size
    threshold that triggers eager flush — why? Then open the `Collector::advance` source — their per-thread
    iteration order matters; why?
  - **Symptom card**: bug shape (pointer freed twice when two bags drained concurrently); rule (bag
    ownership is per-thread; drain is exclusive; advance must scan all bags atomically OR per-thread bag
    must be locked during scan); reapplies at: W33 (the real build), W42 deadlock-detector's retired-graph
    nodes, W37 skiplist node retirement.
  - **Numbers**: predict defer_destroy cost (~5 ns push to bag); advance scan cost (~N threads × 1 cache
    line ≈ ~30-50 ns for N=4 threads); reclaim cost amortized over bag size (~10 ns/pointer).
  - **5-year failure mode**: long-running threads that rarely unpin (e.g. a hot-loop without yield) block
    advance indefinitely → bag growth → OOM. Trigger: a misbehaving consumer in production. Fix:
    cooperative-yield discipline + per-thread retired-bag size cap.
  - **Production handoff**: 3-slot ring bag is the W33 production pattern; W42 deadlock-detector
    reclaims its wait-for graph nodes via this; W37 skiplist retires deleted nodes via this. The bag-
    eviction-ordering you just derived is the silent test at W33's hardened acceptance.
