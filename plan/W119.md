This is a **light career/interview week** — there are no Build exercises and no crate changes. Every item below is a *recall/rehearsal* item: it produces a concrete artifact (a written design doc or mock transcript) and carries a hardened acceptance bar so you can self-grade. The pedagogical point of the week: convert 60+ weeks of lived crate-building into crisp, interview-shaped narratives. The interview tests whether you can *retrieve and structure* what you already built under time pressure — not whether you can learn something new.

**Monday — Systems design: classic problems (canonical warm-up)**

- [ ] **Design drills** → `interview_notes/sd_twitter_feed.md`, `interview_notes/sd_url_shortener.md`, `interview_notes/sd_rate_limiter.md`, `interview_notes/sd_distributed_cache.md` (private). One design per slot, 60 min each, full 4-box structure: (1) functional + non-functional requirements & scale numbers, (2) high-level diagram, (3) deep-dive on the bottleneck component, (4) failure/scaling discussion. (~240 min)
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance**: each doc states a concrete QPS/storage estimate with a back-of-envelope (not "lots of traffic" — e.g. URL shortener: 100M writes/day → ~1.2k writes/s, 100:1 read:write → 120k reads/s, 7-char base62 = 62^7 ≈ 3.5e12 keys); each names the ONE component you'd deep-dive and why it's the bottleneck; rate-limiter doc lists ≥3 algorithms (token bucket / leaky bucket / sliding-window-log) with their tradeoffs.
  - *Mental model*: these four are the "table stakes" the interviewer uses to calibrate before the hard problem. You drill them not to learn distributed systems but to internalize the *4-box ritual* so you spend interview minutes on insight, not on remembering the structure. The Jeff-Dean number muscle from your bar-(c) crate work transfers directly — you already estimate latency budgets reflexively; here you do it out loud.
- [ ] **Output**: 4 files exist under `interview_notes/`, each with the 4 boxes filled and at least one numeric estimate. (~included above)
- [ ] Commit (private notes). (~10 min)

**Tuesday — Systems design: HFT-flavored (your home turf — lead with lived numbers)**

- [ ] **Design drills** → `interview_notes/sd_order_book_server.md`, `interview_notes/sd_market_data_fanout.md`, `interview_notes/sd_fill_router.md` (private). 60 min each. These are NOT generic — anchor every claim to the `matching-engine` (W58), `marketdata-kernelbypass`/`messaging-aeron` (W77+), and `ledger-deterministic` (W90+) crates you actually built. (~180 min)
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance**: order-book doc cites a *real measured* p50/p99 from your matching-engine benches (ns/op, not "fast") and names the single-writer + cache-line-layout decisions you made; market-data fan-out doc explains the Aeron term-buffer / SPMC ring choice and why kernel-bypass (back-of-envelope: syscall ~300ns vs busy-poll ~30ns per message); fill-router doc explains the deterministic-replay / idempotency story from ledger-deterministic. Each doc has a "what I'd do differently at 10× scale" paragraph.
  - *Mental model*: this is the "deep dive into prior work" round in disguise, rehearsed as a design problem. Your differentiator vs other candidates is that you can speak from a *built and benchmarked* system, not a textbook. The trap to avoid: hand-waving with buzzwords when you have real numbers — interviewers at destination-tier HFT firms will probe one level deeper than the buzzword. Pre-loading the real ns figures here means you answer reflexively in W126-W129.
- [ ] **Output**: 3 files exist, each citing ≥1 real benchmark number from your own crates and ≥1 mechanical-sympathy decision (cache line / single-writer / zero-alloc / back-pressure). (~included above)

**Wednesday — Systems design: distributed (consistency & consensus vocabulary)**

- [ ] **Design drills** → `interview_notes/sd_distributed_kv.md`, `interview_notes/sd_vector_search.md`, `interview_notes/sd_settlement_ledger.md` (private). 60 min each. Anchor to `mini-db`/`lsm-core` (W40+), `vector-db` (W100+), `ledger-deterministic` + `consensus-vsr`/`consensus-raft` (W90-W117). (~180 min)
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance**: distributed-KV doc states its consistency model explicitly (linearizable vs eventual) and the partitioning + replication scheme (consistent hashing, quorum R+W>N); vector-search doc gives the ANN index choice (HNSW/IVF) with a recall-vs-latency tradeoff number and a back-of-envelope on memory for N vectors × D dims × 4 bytes; settlement-ledger doc explains how consensus (VSR/Raft) gives ordered, durable, deterministic commits and names the failure model it tolerates (f of 2f+1). Each doc names the CAP tradeoff it chose and why.
  - *Mental model*: this round tests whether you have the *vocabulary* of distributed systems to map your low-level crate knowledge onto interview-shaped questions. You built consensus and a deterministic ledger — the gap to close is naming the textbook concepts (quorum, linearizability, leader election) that interviewers expect. DDIA ch.5-9 is the lookup reference if a term is fuzzy; do not read it cover to cover.
- [ ] **Output**: 3 files exist, each naming a consistency model, a partition/replication scheme, and a concrete CAP/failure-model tradeoff. (~included above)
- [ ] Commit (private notes). (~10 min)

**Thursday — Mock: full 4-round on-site simulation (timed, no pausing)**

- [ ] **Coding round (Hard)** → `interview_notes/mock_coding_<date>.md`. 60 min, one Hard problem (LeetCode-Hard tier or a lock-free/concurrency problem from your wheelhouse), strict timer, narrate aloud. (~60 min)
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance**: a working solution committed with stated time/space complexity, AND a written note of where you stalled and the recovery move. A solution that compiles but you couldn't explain the complexity = fail.
- [ ] **Systems design round** → `interview_notes/mock_sd_<date>.md`. 60 min, one fresh problem you did NOT drill Mon-Wed, timed. (~60 min)
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance**: the 4-box structure completed within 60 min with ≥2 numeric estimates and an explicit bottleneck deep-dive. Run out of time on the deep-dive = fail; that's the signal to drill pacing.
- [ ] **Behavioral round** → `interview_notes/mock_behavioral_<date>.md`. 60 min, rehearse solo against STAR-formatted stories. (~60 min)
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance**: ≥5 STAR stories written (conflict, failure, leadership, ambiguity, biggest-impact) each ≤2 min spoken; biggest-impact story leads with a quantified result.
- [ ] **Deep-dive-into-prior-work round** → `interview_notes/mock_deepdive_<date>.md`. 60 min, present one crate (matching-engine or ledger-deterministic) end to end: problem → design → tradeoffs → measured result → what you'd change. (~60 min)
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance**: a 10-min spoken walkthrough that survives 3 levels of "why" on the single-writer or memory-ordering decision; if the third "why" bottoms out in hand-waving, note it and re-study that crate's notes.
  - *Mental model*: the full 4-round simulation is the dress rehearsal for W126-W129 real loops. Doing all four back-to-back under a timer reveals the real failure mode — fatigue and pacing — that single-problem practice hides. Use AI as the interviewer for coding/SD prompts and probing follow-ups; rehearse behavioral and deep-dive solo (recorded), since those are about *your* lived material.
- [ ] **Output**: 4 mock transcripts under `interview_notes/`, each with a self-graded pass/fail against its acceptance bar and one concrete drill-this note. (~included above)
- [ ] Commit (private notes). (~10 min)
