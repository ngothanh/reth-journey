## Tuesday — Operationalize the §6 ecosystem-selection filter into a scoring rubric

- [ ] **Build the filter instrument (not a venue audit yet — the *tool*)**: `notes/116_ecosystem_filter_rubric.md` — turn §6's seven pass/fail criteria into a falsifiable scoring rubric you can apply to any venue and that a future-you (or a skeptic) would score the same way. (~total 120 min)
  - 🔧 **Why this matters**: §6 says *"a bet is GO only if all seven pass; otherwise it is a bridge job, not a bet."* That sentence is the single most expensive decision instrument in the plan — a false GO on a bet costs years (§7: each bet upgrades the next, so a wasted bet poisons the whole sequence). A vague rubric produces motivated reasoning: a venue you *like* gets scored GO on a maybe. The rubric's job is to make the seven criteria **conservative by construction** — each criterion has an explicit "what counts as PASS" bar that a tempting-but-wrong venue fails. This is the W131 Bet-#1-decision instrument, calibrated now while there's no live offer pressuring the score.
  - 🎯 **Hardened acceptance** — the rubric must, for EACH of the seven §6 criteria, specify:
    1. **Founding-tier allocation** — PASS bar: equity/token grant in the founding-engineer band (explicit %-or-token-count threshold you set). NAME the anti-pattern that fails it: *Airwallex — late-stage, tiny employee equity → never a bet.* "Generous-for-an-employee" is a FAIL, not a PASS.
    2. **Tier-1 backing** — PASS bar: ≥1 of {Paradigm, a16z crypto, Dragonfly, Polychain, 1kx, Multicoin, Jump, Wintermute} on the cap table. A tier-2 fund alone is a FAIL.
    3. **Core-criticality** — PASS bar: you build the engine that *is* the company (matching/risk/settlement core), provable from the role description. "Peripheral integration / SDK / growth-eng" is a FAIL. (This is the criterion the flagship blog directly serves.)
    4. **Multi-billion-FDV ceiling** — PASS bar: TAM + named comparables (Hyperliquid, dYdX, GMX, Variational, Ostium) support a ≥$1B FDV outcome.
    5. **Token reality** — PASS bar: pre-TGE entry; sane vesting/cliff (≈1y cliff / 4y vest); entry FDV leaves ≥10× headroom; no predatory unlock dumping on early contributors.
    6. **Remote-native** — PASS bar: team remote-distributed; Vietnam-remote works; no colo/on-site requirement. (Note: an on-site requirement here interacts with the §7 geography flip-trigger — record it, don't auto-fail, but it is a FAIL on *this* criterion.)
    7. **Cycle timing** — PASS bar: entering seed–Series B / pre-TGE with a plausible market-cycle tailwind inside the vesting window.
  - 🎯 **Each criterion gets an explicit DATA SOURCE** (where you'd actually verify it: cap-table from a funding announcement / Crunchbase / the fund's portfolio page for #2; role JD + a founder conversation for #3; TGE status + tokenomics doc for #5). A criterion you can't source is scored UNKNOWN = treated as FAIL until sourced (conservative default).
  - 🎯 **The GO rule, stated unambiguously**: GO requires **7/7 PASS**. **6/7 with one UNKNOWN = NOT GO (it's a bridge candidate + a due-diligence item)**. The rubric must state this in one sentence so W131 has no wiggle room.

  #### The pre-mortem (load-bearing — write it before scoring any venue)

  - 🎯 **Pre-mortem prompt** — write sealed answers in `notes/116_filter_premortem.md`, then keep them honest against the rubric:
    - **PM1 (the motivated-GO trap)**: 4-step trace of how you talk yourself into a false GO. Step 1: a venue has a *charismatic founder + exciting product*. Step 2: criterion 1 (allocation) is a "we'll figure out the grant later" maybe. Step 3: you score it PASS "because the team is clearly going to win." Step 4: 18 months later the grant lands at employee-tier and the bet was never a bet. **The fix the rubric must encode**: allocation-maybe = FAIL; "exciting" is not a criterion. State which criterion is most vulnerable to this (it is #1 and #5 — the ones founders defer).
    - **PM2 (the tier-1-halo trap)**: 3-step trace where a Paradigm logo (criterion 2 PASS) makes you wave through criteria 3 and 5. The fix: every criterion is scored *independently*; #2 PASS does not lend credibility to #3. State the rule.
    - **PM3 (the UNKNOWN-as-PASS trap — the worst one)**: 3-step trace where you can't source token reality (criterion 5) before a deadline, score it PASS to keep the venue alive, and place a bet on a predatory unlock. The fix: UNKNOWN = FAIL until sourced; a deadline pressuring you to score UNKNOWN-as-PASS is itself the signal to NOT bet yet. This is the conservative-by-construction rule ported from `risk-engine`'s haircut: under-credit, never over-credit.

  #### Failure cards (the ways the rubric gets misused — label + Symptom/Why/Fix/Cost-avoided/Muscle)

  - **(NEWBIE) Scoring "the product is great" as a criterion.**
    - Symptom: a venue scores GO on energy/excitement; none of the seven criteria actually moved.
    - Why: product enthusiasm is the most available signal and the seven criteria are deliberately *not* about product quality — they're about whether the *bet structure* is sound (allocation, backing, criticality, FDV, token, remote, timing).
    - Fix: the rubric has exactly seven rows; "product excitement" is not a row. Score only the rows.
    - Cost avoided: a years-long bet placed on the wrong axis (great product, employee-tier allocation = no upside capture).
    - Muscle: the bet thesis is *structural*, not *aesthetic*. Reapplies at: W126 first founding-eng convos, W131 Bet #1 decision.
  - **(SENIOR) Treating 6/7 as "basically a GO."**
    - Symptom: a venue passes six criteria; you round up to GO because "the seventh is probably fine."
    - Why: GO is conjunctive (all seven) for a reason — the failing criterion is usually the load-bearing one (allocation or token reality), and "probably fine" is the motivated-reasoning tell.
    - Fix: the GO rule is 7/7. 6/7 routes to *bridge candidate + diligence item*, never GO. Encode it as a hard gate.
    - Cost avoided: the exact §6 failure mode the checklist exists to prevent ("otherwise it is a bridge job, not a bet").
    - Muscle: conjunctive gates don't round up. Reapplies at: W117 M30 gate (capstone v1.0 AND cluster-VOPR AND 2000h AND shortlist≥5 AND ≥2 convos — all required), W131.
  - **(EXPERT) Letting the rubric drift to fit a venue you've already emotionally committed to.**
    - Symptom: after a great founder conversation, the PASS bars quietly soften ("founding-band, well, *senior-founding-band* counts").
    - Why: the rubric was calibrated cold (now), but it's *applied* warm (W126+) under social and FOMO pressure; an un-versioned rubric silently mutates.
    - Fix: freeze and date this rubric file; W126/W131 score against *this* version, and any change to a PASS bar is a logged, dated diff with a rationale — never an in-the-moment edit. (Same discipline as the v1.0 interface lock: the shape is frozen so later extensions are deliberate, not accidental.)
    - Cost avoided: a bet placed against a rubric that was retrofitted to justify the bet — i.e. no filter at all.
    - Muscle: calibrate decision instruments cold, freeze them, apply them warm. Reapplies at: W131 Bet #1 decision, every subsequent bet (§7).

  - **5-year failure mode** (write in `notes/116_filter_followup.md`): when Bet #1 has shipped and reputation + network from it raise the reachable tier (§7: "each bet upgrades the next"), the rubric's PASS bars (especially #1 allocation-band and #4 FDV-ceiling) should *ratchet up* for Bet #2 — what was a PASS-tier allocation for a first-time founding eng is below-bar for a proven one. State the migration: version the rubric per bet; Bet #2's #1 and #4 thresholds inherit from Bet #1's *realized* outcome, not the original constants. Trigger: Bet #1 placed (W131+) and a vest milestone reached.
